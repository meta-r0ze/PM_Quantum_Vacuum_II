\section{Conclusion}

Persistence Mechanics motivates an economical description of the work needed to maintain a system's required distinctions. For explicit mathematical models, Lean now verifies survival-weight concentration, phase-covariant transport, a dispersion/Clifford equivalence, scalar sign selection and finite-dimensional routes to norm-preserving evolution. These results expose their state, symmetry, cost and limiting assumptions.

The concrete advances now include the $D_4$ triangle area tensor and controlled Abelian
constant-field limit, actual glue-class carrier/reflection event counts, conditional
phase-sensitive trace/cosine costs and a controlled link expansion. The specified
Wilson/domain-wall Hamiltonian has a checked signed spectrum; its local unitary completion
preserves the known vacuum modes. Exclusion of additional finite-tick modes remains open.
The central priority is to connect the actual carrier dynamics, primitive event law and
fermion/gauge/scalar responses in one model, then extend its continuum estimates.

The physical carrier identification, common response normalization, gauge representations and numerical coefficients remain to be derived. The gravitational action and the measurement interpretation remain proposals. Finite capacity is not treated as a proof of exact operator exhaustion or a resolution of the hierarchy problem. Paper III's constants must be tested against the same response once it is constructed; agreement of separately proposed formulas does not establish joint closure.

The program therefore has a precise next step: connect the lattice update, its spectrum and its observable response in one model. That would turn the current operator-level and geometric results into stronger physical claims, with explicit conditions for their validation or failure.
